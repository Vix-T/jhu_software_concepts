Complex Network Analysis of Russian Translations

To address the challenge in translated literature research, where a translator's individual style is obscured by the original author's semantic content, this project applies complex network analysis alongside classical stylometry to help define the subtle styles of five Russian translations of Mark Twain's The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1911-1960). The computational analysis revealed statistically significant divergences in both lexical and topological profiles, confirming that translators retain distinct signatures despite sharing a source text. Classical metrics indicate a reduction in lexical diversity over time, identifying the 1911 Engel'gardt translation as an outlier with the highest vocabulary richness compared to the statistically repetitive profiles of the 1949 Braude and 1960 Daruzes texts. Structural analysis discovered topological anomalies: the 1960 Daruzes translation uniquely inverts the standard grammatical hierarchy by prioritizing verbs over nouns. Finally, betweenness centrality analysis found a wide range of data when ranking the most connective nodes across the corpus, suggesting that different translators had quite different preferences for which stop words they relied upon.

Stylometric Analysis of Mark Twain's The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn in Russian Translation

Dr. Cassio de Oliveira, a professor of Russian literature at Portland State University, was writing a book on Mark Twain's reception in the Soviet Union and needed quantitative support for his qualitative analysis of five Russian translations spanning 1911-1960. As lead of a five-person consulting team through PSU's Computation and Data-Enabled Science NSF Research Training Group, I directed the technical execution of a stylometric analysis using Burrows' Delta method, building an R-based NLP pipeline (udpipe, tm, proxy, factoextra) to lemmatize, vectorize, and compare the five translations by word frequency, part-of-speech patterns, and lexical diversity. The pipeline used PCA for dimensionality reduction and cosine similarity to quantify stylistic distance between every pair of translations, producing similarity matrices and cluster visualizations Dr. de Oliveira could cite directly in his book. Beyond the technical build, I led team workflow: since our program hadn't covered version control, I brought in an outside Git consultant to train the team on collaborative coding practices, and ensured each member understood the analysis deeply enough to present it confidently to the client.
